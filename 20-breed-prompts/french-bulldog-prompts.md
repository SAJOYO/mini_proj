# French Bulldog Mascot — 4 Life Stage Prompts

> Ready to send as-is. Pose, framing and gaze are already written in.
> Human in the scene is gender-neutral ("a person", they/them).
> Their clothing, hairstyle and appearance are deliberately unspecified -
> only their pose, their face and their contact with the dog are
> described. The person's face is required to be fully in frame.

## Palette (fawn)

```
  Body coat        #C79A70
  Chest / underside#EBD8BE
  Muzzle mask      #7A6047
  Nose/eyes/mouth  #241E1A
  Outline          #5C4632
```

## Breed Anchors (never swap in other breeds' wording)

- Huge ERECT rounded BAT EARS, broad at the base and set high and wide - the single most identifying feature
- A FLAT pushed-in face: a very short broad muzzle, a deep stop, and soft rolled wrinkles above the nose
- Compact, heavy-boned, square body with a broad chest and a slightly roached back - small but solid, never dainty
- Very short smooth coat, a dark mask over the muzzle, and a tiny low SCREW TAIL

## Poses Used Here

| Stage | Pose |
|---|---|
| Puppy | rolled over belly up |
| Adolescent | front paws up on the knee |
| Young adult | floor sprawl |
| Senior | drowsing in the person's lap |

## Notes

- The bat ears carry the breed. If they come out folded, drooping or pointed, repeat "huge erect rounded bat ears, broad at the base, set high and wide" at the very end of the prompt.
- Keep the build SOLID. Any "dainty" or "slender" wording turns it into a Boston Terrier. The Frenchie is small but heavy-boned with a broad chest.
- Keep the wrinkles SOFT and few - one or two rolls above the nose. Heavy wrinkling turns it into a Pug or an English Bulldog.
- Do not put a dog-eye-level camera instruction back in - it fights the face requirement and crops the person's head.
- To reshuffle the poses, swap only the paragraph that starts right after "THE DOG:". Ten alternates are in pose-library.txt.
