# Golden Retriever Mascot — 4 Life Stage Prompts

> Ready to send as-is. Pose and framing are already written in.
> Human in the scene is gender-neutral ("a person", they/them).
> Their clothing, hairstyle and appearance are deliberately unspecified -
> only their pose, their face and their contact with the dog are
> described. The person's face is required to be fully in frame.

## Palette

```
  Body coat        #E5B168
  Feathering /
  chest / muzzle   #F4DCB0
  Ears             #CE9550
  Nose / eyes /
  mouth            #4A3728
  Outline          #7A5636
```

## Breed Anchors (must never be swapped for Bichon/Poodle wording)

- VISIBLE drop ears  (NOT "no visible ears" = Bichon)
- Smooth flowing / feathered outline (NOT "scalloped" = Bichon, NOT "tight curls" = Poodle)
- Long plumed tail  (NOT "cotton-puff tail", NOT "pom-pom")

## Poses Used Here

| Stage | Pose |
|---|---|
| Puppy | carried against the chest |
| Adolescent | front paws up on the knee |
| Young adult | sitting side by side, seen from behind |
| Senior | leaning its weight against the leg |

## 1. Puppy / 유아기

```text
35mm film-look vector composited in.
A person on a sunlit wooden floor by a window, close beside the dog.
Sheer curtain light, dust motes, warm morning sunlight, blurred potted
plant far in the background, fine grain.

Both the person and the dog are fully inside the frame together, both
in sharp focus on the same focal plane, physically touching or within
arm's reach - this is a photo of the two of them, not a dog photo with
someone in the background. THE PERSON'S FACE MUST BE FULLY VISIBLE:
their whole head, chin to crown, sits inside the frame, never cropped
at the neck, at the shoulders or by the top edge. Pull the framing wide
enough to hold the person's head and the dog at the same time. The dog
is the main subject, but the person's face, body and hands are clearly
visible and part of the picture, never a blurred shape behind. Only the
far background falls out of focus.

GAZE - unforced and natural. The person is either looking down at the
dog with a soft expression or glancing toward the camera. The dog is
either looking up at the person or giving the camera a relaxed, candid
glance with its head tilted off-axis. The two eyelines must make sense
together. If either one looks at the camera it reads as a casual
snapshot glance, never a stiff, symmetrical, front-facing portrait
stare.

THE DOG: a Golden Retriever puppy
Held up against the person's chest, scooped under the front legs, the
body hanging soft and relaxed against them. The person's face, arms and
upper body are all in frame, their cheek close to the puppy's head. The
puppy's head rests back against their shoulder, its face right beside
theirs, giving the camera a soft sleepy glance. Limp, content, unposed.
Oversized rounded head (1.3x body width), broad round skull with a very
short blunt muzzle. VISIBLE EARS: soft floppy drop ears hanging against
the cheeks, rounded tips, oversized for the head. Smooth unbroken
outline, short plush fuzzy puppy coat with NO feathering yet. Plump
round belly, no waist, comically oversized paws. Huge wide-set round
dark brown eyes #4A3728 with a white highlight dot upper-right, small
rounded muzzle in pale cream, small dark oval nose, tiny closed mouth.
Very short neck, soft rounded shoulders, small cream chest patch, short
thick legs, short stubby tail with no plume yet. Innocent, curious,
uncoordinated.
Coat pale golden #F0C88A; muzzle, chest, paws cream #F4DCB0; ears
slightly deeper gold #CE9550; nose, eyes, mouth #4A3728; one uniform
thick warm brown outline #7A5636.
```

## Notes

- The face-cropping fix: the old framing block said "medium shot at roughly the dog's eye level", which pushed the person's head above the top edge. That line is gone, replaced by "pull the framing wide enough to hold the person's head and the dog at the same time". Do not put a dog-eye-level camera instruction back in - it fights the face requirement.
- If a face still gets cropped, append "wide shot, the person's whole head visible, nothing cropped at the top of the frame" at the very end of the prompt.
- The GAZE paragraph deliberately allows a glance at the camera. It is the stiff, symmetrical, front-facing stare that looks wrong, not eye contact itself - that distinction is spelled out in the paragraph, so keep the whole thing if you edit it.
- The young adult pose used to be shot from BEHIND, which hid both faces. It is now a three-quarter FRONT angle with both looking off to the side, so the companionable feel survives but the faces show.
- To reshuffle the poses, swap only the paragraph that starts right after "THE DOG:". Ten alternates are in pose-library.txt. Substitute exactly one - never paste the list and ask the model to choose, it blends them instead of picking.
- A full-grown Golden Retriever is too large to be picked up. Keep the "carried" pose on the puppy stage only.
- The puppy coat is intentionally paler (#F0C88A) - real Golden Retrievers darken with age. Set every stage to #E5B168 instead if all four images must read as the same individual dog.
- If the senior's white mask comes out too strong, change "distinct" to "subtle".
- Image models lean toward one gender even when the prompt only says "a person". If results skew, try "a person of any gender" or keep only hands/legs in frame.
