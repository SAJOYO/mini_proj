# Toy Poodle Mascot — 4 Life Stage Prompts

> Ready to send as-is. Pose and framing are already written in.
> Human in the scene is gender-neutral ("a person", they/them).
> Their clothing, hairstyle and appearance are deliberately unspecified -
> only their pose, their face and their contact with the dog are
> described. The person's face is required to be fully in frame.

## Palette (apricot toy poodle)

```
  Body coat        #D89A6A
  Deep curls/ears  #B87A4E
  Muzzle / chest   #EFCBA6
  Nose/eyes/mouth  #3E2A20
  Outline          #6B4630
```

## Breed Anchors (never swap in other breeds' wording)

- Tightly coiled curls / springy ringlets (NOT "scalloped" = Bichon, NOT "feathering" = Golden Retriever)
- Round TOPKNOT of curls on the head
- Long curly drop ears framing the face
- Slim leggy build, tail ending in a round POM-POM

## Poses Used Here

| Stage | Pose |
|---|---|
| Puppy | asleep in the person's lap |
| Adolescent | rolled over belly up |
| Young adult | floor sprawl (the original "푸들 포즈.PNG" reference) |
| Senior | carried against the chest |

## 1. Puppy / 유아기

```text
35mm film-look vector composited in.
A person on a soft cream rug in warm morning window light, close beside
the dog. Blurred wooden floor far behind, dust motes in the light,
fine grain.

Both the person and the dog are fully inside the frame together, both
in sharp focus on the same focal plane, physically touching or within
arm's reach - this is a photo of the two of them, not a dog photo with
someone in the background. THE PERSON'S FACE MUST BE FULLY VISIBLE:
their whole head, chin to crown, sits inside the frame, never cropped
at the neck, at the shoulders or by the top edge. Pull the framing wide
enough to hold the person's head and the dog at the same time. The dog
is the main subject, but the person's face, body and hands are clearly
visible and part of the picture, never a blurred shape behind. Only the
far background falls out of focus.

GAZE - unforced and natural. The person is either looking down at the
dog with a soft expression or glancing toward the camera. The dog is
either looking up at the person or giving the camera a relaxed, candid
glance with its head tilted off-axis. The two eyelines must make sense
together. If either one looks at the camera it reads as a casual
snapshot glance, never a stiff, symmetrical, front-facing portrait
stare.

THE DOG: a Toy Poodle puppy
Curled up drowsing in the person's lap, body folded into a tight comma,
head tucked against their forearm, one paw hanging loose over their
knee. The person sits upright, NOT hunched over the dog, and only tips
their chin down a little so their face stays open to the camera. The
dog's eyes are heavy-lidded and half open, drifting up toward them.
Sleepy, heavy, unposed.

COMPOSITION: vertical portrait crop. The person's head sits near the
top of the frame with clear space above it, and the puppy curled in
their lap sits near the bottom. The frame runs from above the crown of
the person's head down past their lap, so BOTH the whole face and the
whole puppy are inside it at once. Never show the person as a headless
or faceless torso, and never crop the top of their head.
Oversized rounded head (1.3x body width) with a very short blunt muzzle
and a soft baby-round skull. VISIBLE EARS: short floppy drop ears
hanging against the cheeks, covered in loose baby waves. Coat is soft
crimped puppy fluff, gentle loose waves not yet tight curls. Small
barely-formed topknot of fluff. Plump round belly, oversized paws.
Large wide-set round dark eyes #3E2A20, small cream muzzle, small dark
oval nose, tiny closed mouth. Very short neck, soft rounded shoulders,
short stubby legs, short tail with a tiny tuft at the tip. Innocent,
drowsy, uncoordinated.
Coat pale apricot #E4B187; muzzle, chest, paws cream #EFCBA6; ears
deeper apricot #C98F63; nose, eyes, mouth #3E2A20; one uniform thick
warm brown outline #6B4630.
```

## Notes

- The face-cropping fix: the old framing block said "medium shot at roughly the dog's eye level", which pushed the person's head above the top edge. That line is gone, replaced by "pull the framing wide enough to hold the person's head and the dog at the same time". Do not put a dog-eye-level camera instruction back in - it fights the face requirement.
- If the face still gets cropped, append "wide shot, the person's whole head visible, nothing cropped at the top of the frame" at the very end of the prompt.
- The GAZE paragraph deliberately allows a glance at the camera. It is the stiff, symmetrical, front-facing stare that looks wrong, not eye contact itself - that distinction is spelled out in the paragraph, so keep the whole thing if you edit it.
- To reshuffle the poses, swap only the paragraph that starts right after "THE DOG:". Ten alternates are in pose-library.txt. Substitute exactly one - never paste the list and ask the model to choose, it blends them instead of picking.
- Color swaps: black #3A3532 / #5A5350, cream #EFE3D2 / #D8C4AB, red-brown #A8603A / #8A4B2C.
- If the muzzle comes out too long and pointed, change "slim, gently tapering muzzle" to "short rounded teddy-bear muzzle" - that gives the teddy-bear cut common in Korea.
- Image models lean toward one gender even when the prompt only says "a person". If results skew, try "a person of any gender" or keep only hands/legs in frame.
