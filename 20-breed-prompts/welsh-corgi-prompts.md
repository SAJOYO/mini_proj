# Pembroke Welsh Corgi Mascot — 4 Life Stage Prompts

> Ready to send as-is. Pose, framing and gaze are already written in.
> Human in the scene is gender-neutral ("a person", they/them).
> Their clothing, hairstyle and appearance are deliberately unspecified -
> only their pose, their face and their contact with the dog are
> described. The person's face is required to be fully in frame.

## Palette (red and white)

```
  Body coat        #C98B4E
  White markings   #F5EDE0
  Ears / shading   #A96C36
  Nose/eyes/mouth  #2C2420
  Outline          #6B4A2E
```

## Breed Anchors (never swap in other breeds' wording)

- LONG LOW body on very short sturdy legs, but heavy-boned and barrel-chested, not slender (a Dachshund is longer and finer)
- Oversized ERECT ears, wide at the base and ROUNDED at the tips, standing straight up
- Fox-like face with a white blaze running up the muzzle between the eyes
- A big round fluffy rump with a short bobtail; thick medium double coat with a pale cream underside

## Poses Used Here

| Stage | Pose |
|---|---|
| Puppy | front paws up on the knee |
| Adolescent | play bow, about to spring |
| Young adult | walking side by side |
| Senior | floor sprawl |

```text
plant far in the background, fine grain.

Both the person and the dog are fully inside the frame together, both
in sharp focus on the same focal plane, physically touching or within
arm's reach - this is a photo of the two of them, not a dog photo with
someone in the background. THE PERSON'S FACE MUST BE FULLY VISIBLE:
their whole head, chin to crown, sits inside the frame, never cropped
at the neck, at the shoulders or by the top edge. Pull the framing wide
enough to hold the person's head and the dog at the same time. The dog
is the main subject, but the person's face, body and hands are clearly
visible and part of the picture, never a blurred shape behind. Only the
far background falls out of focus.

GAZE - unforced and natural. The person is either looking down at the
dog with a soft expression or glancing toward the camera. The dog is
either looking up at the person or giving the camera a relaxed, candid
glance with its head tilted off-axis. The two eyelines must make sense
together. If either one looks at the camera it reads as a casual
snapshot glance, never a stiff, symmetrical, front-facing portrait
stare.

THE DOG: a Pembroke Welsh Corgi puppy
Standing on its hind legs with both front paws planted on the person's
knee, stretching upward, body fully extended and slightly off-balance.
The person bends toward it, both hands reaching in, their face in frame
and laughing down at it. The dog's head is tilted up and back, gaze
locked on their face. Eager, pushy, unposed.
Oversized rounded head (1.3x body width) with a short blunt fox muzzle.
Ears large, soft and ROUNDED, still flopping over rather than standing
upright - only just starting to lift. Body already long and low, a
round barrel on stubby paddle legs with oversized paws. Thick soft
puppy double coat standing out all over. Huge round dark eyes #2C2420
with a white highlight dot upper-right, small dark oval nose, tiny
closed mouth. A white blaze up the muzzle, white chest and paws, big
round fluffy rump with a tiny bobtail. Innocent, roly-poly,
uncoordinated.
Coat red #C98B4E; blaze, chest, paws, underside white #F5EDE0; ears and
shading #A96C36; nose, eyes, mouth #2C2420; one uniform thick warm
brown outline #6B4A2E.
```

## Notes

- This is the PEMBROKE (bobtail, rounded ears). For the Cardigan variety swap in "a long full brush tail carried low" and "very large rounded ears set wide" - and change it in all four stages.
- Do not let the body get as long and fine as a Dachshund. The Corgi is heavy-boned and barrel-chested; keep "heavy-boned, barrel-chested, big round fluffy rump" in the prompt.
- Do not put a dog-eye-level camera instruction back in - it fights the face requirement and crops the person's head.
- To reshuffle the poses, swap only the paragraph that starts right after "THE DOG:". Ten alternates are in pose-library.txt.
